# The weekend sweep

mlspend is a portfolio demonstration. Its platform costs are simulated with known truth. Its portfolio costs are shadow estimates of public project usage at published list prices; nobody paid these amounts. No figure is a quote, an invoice or financial advice.

## Impact

A planted retry amplification increased research training demand. The incident-removal counterfactual isolates its platform-level effect; it is not summed into overlapping action savings.

## Cause and detection

The simulator deliberately increased retrying training work. The method did not read the planted schedule: it consumed the observed cost series and development-calibrated seasonal residuals. Evaluation alone joined alerts to the incident window. The zero-delay result reflects the observation timestamp and synthetic step change; it is not a production response-time guarantee. Daily budget values become observable at the end of the day, and a critical budget flag does not identify a retry cause.

## Corrective experiment

Bound retries, checkpoint interrupted work and inspect whole-node release before treating a cheaper rate as savings. The existing counterfactual evaluator measures independent and joint actions. A production remediation was not performed. The optional budget refusal service remains outside this release.

## What would change the conclusion

Delayed billing arrival, unknown incident boundaries, smaller gradual changes and missing provider metadata can worsen detection. The low alert precision alongside high event recall is retained rather than hidden by a favorable incident-only score.
